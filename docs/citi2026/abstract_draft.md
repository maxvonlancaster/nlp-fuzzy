# Evaluating Unsupervised Extractive Summarization of Medical Transcriptions: The Role of Positional Reference Bias

Automatic evaluation of extractive summarisation relies on ROUGE scores against reference texts, yet the rankings it produces may depend on how references are structured relative to source documents. We compare five unsupervised extractive methods (Lead-3, Random, TextRank, LexRank and LSA) on 1,000 cleaned MTSamples transcriptions, selecting three sentences per summary and scoring against the short `description` field, which we treat as a proxy reference rather than a gold-standard summary. Lead-3 achieves the highest mean ROUGE-1 (0.309), followed by LexRank (0.224), TextRank (0.207), LSA (0.170) and Random (0.156). Paired t-tests and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm correction show that the Lead-versus-LexRank gap is large (dz = -0.45), whereas LexRank-versus-TextRank (dz = 0.12) is small, and the LSA-versus-Random difference is significant under the t-test but not the Wilcoxon test, with negligible effect size. Among 864 documents with at least 200 words, description tokens overlap the first 100 words far more than the last 100 (mean recall 0.861 versus 0.357), and this positional bias correlates with Lead's advantage over LexRank (partial Spearman rho = 0.26 after controlling for document and reference length). These findings are associational and specific to this corpus. They suggest that benchmark rankings on proxy references should be interpreted alongside the positional structure of those references.
