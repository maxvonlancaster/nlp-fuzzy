# Final Statistical Summary: Paired ROUGE-1 Comparisons

Written for: CITI-2026 reviewers familiar with ROUGE and paired tests.

Source: `results/citi2026_final/tables/per_document_scores.csv` (1,000 cleaned MTSamples documents, five methods, seed 42), aligned by document SHA-256. Generated by `scripts/analyse_final_statistics.py`; full table in `results/citi2026_final/tables/paired_statistics.csv`. This supersedes `statistical_summary.md` (from the earlier run), whose Random baseline predates the seeding fix and is stale.

Differences are M2 minus M1: positive means M2 scores higher. Holm correction is applied over the 10 comparisons, separately for the paired t-test and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (alpha 0.05). 95% CIs come from 2,000 paired bootstrap resamples (seed 42). Cohen's dz uses the standard deviation of within-document differences.

## Highlighted comparisons

| Comparison | Mean diff (M2 - M1) | 95% CI | p (t, Holm) | p (Wilcoxon, Holm) | Cohen dz |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lead vs LexRank | -0.0844 (Lead higher) | [-0.0966, -0.0732] | 2.75e-40 | 1.03e-42 | -0.445 |
| LexRank vs TextRank | +0.0173 (LexRank higher) | [+0.0088, +0.0258] | 1.86e-4 | 1.10e-4 | +0.124 |
| LSA vs Random | +0.0140 (LSA higher) | [+0.0047, +0.0232] | 3.57e-3 | 1.35e-1 | +0.092 |

## Interpretation

- **Lead vs LexRank.** Lead is higher by 8.4 points. Both tests agree, and the effect is medium (dz = -0.45). This is the most robust difference in the study.
- **LexRank vs TextRank.** LexRank is higher by 1.7 points. Both tests are significant, but dz = 0.12 is small. The result is statistically clear and practically modest.
- **LSA vs Random.** LSA is higher by 1.4 points, which reverses the earlier run. The paired t-test is significant, but the Wilcoxon test is not after Holm correction (p = 0.135), and dz = 0.09 is negligible. Treat this as no reliable difference.

## Significance versus practical effect

Significance with n = 1,000 is easy to reach for differences of about 1 point. Use dz and the CI width to judge practical importance; the LSA vs Random result shows why the t-test alone is insufficient. Zero within-document differences are common (26 to 143 documents per comparison, e.g. identical summaries from two methods) and were handled by Wilcoxon's default zero method ("wilcox", which drops them); no comparison lacked non-zero differences.

## Limitations

- ROUGE-1 only; ROUGE-2 and ROUGE-L are not tested here.
- Random uses a single document-derived seed scheme; variation across base seeds is not assessed.
- One dataset (cleaned MTSamples) and one summary length (3 sentences).
