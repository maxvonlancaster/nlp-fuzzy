# Statistical Summary: Pairwise ROUGE-1 Comparisons

Written for: CITI-2026 reviewers familiar with ROUGE and paired tests.

Source: `results/citi2026_clean/tables/pairwise_comparisons.csv` (paired t-test, 1000 cleaned MTSamples documents, seed 42) and `results/citi2026_clean/evaluation_results.json`. Holm step-down correction over all 10 comparisons, alpha = 0.05, output in `results/citi2026_clean/tables/pairwise_holm.csv` (generated by `scripts/analyse_significance.py`).

## Results

All 10 pairwise differences remain significant after Holm correction (all adjusted p < 0.01). The difference is defined as Method 2 minus Method 1; a negative value means Method 1 scores higher.

| Comparison | Mean diff (M2 - M1) | Higher | p (Holm) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead vs LexRank | -0.0844 | Lead | 2.8e-40 |
| TextRank vs LexRank | +0.0173 | LexRank | 1.9e-4 |
| Random vs LSA | -0.0222 | Random | 1.5e-4 |
| Lead vs Random | -0.1165 | Lead | 2.1e-67 |
| Lead vs TextRank | -0.1017 | Lead | 5.3e-57 |
| Lead vs LSA | -0.1387 | Lead | 4.5e-92 |
| Random vs TextRank | +0.0148 | TextRank | 6.7e-3 |
| Random vs LexRank | +0.0321 | LexRank | 4.1e-8 |
| TextRank vs LSA | -0.0370 | TextRank | 5.7e-16 |
| LSA vs LexRank | +0.0543 | LexRank | 2.0e-24 |

## Interpretation of the highlighted comparisons

- **Lead vs LexRank.** Lead is higher by 8.4 ROUGE-1 points, the largest gap among methods that are not Random or LSA. This is the strongest practical difference in the table, consistent with the positional-overlap diagnostic (Task 2.3) showing that descriptions draw heavily on opening text.
- **TextRank vs LexRank.** LexRank is higher by 1.7 points. The result is statistically significant after correction, but the gap is small and sits within the range of implementation choices (threshold, tie-breaking) that differ between the two methods. Treat it as a modest difference, not an established ranking.
- **Random vs LSA.** Random is higher by 2.2 points, so the LSA method scores below a random baseline on this metric. The effect is significant, but it is a practical concern for the LSA configuration (5 components, sentence selection), not evidence that random selection is a good summariser.

## Significance versus effect size

- Significance here is driven by sample size: with n = 1000 paired documents, differences of about 1 ROUGE-1 point reach p < 0.01. Significance alone does not establish practical importance.
- The effect sizes reported are raw mean differences in ROUGE-1 (F-score scale, 0 to 1). A paired standardised effect size (e.g. Cohen's d_z) needs per-document score differences, which are not in the two input files, so it is not reported here.
- Holm correction controls the family-wise error rate across the 10 comparisons; it does not address dependence between comparisons that share a method.

## Limitations

- Only ROUGE-1 is tested for significance; ROUGE-2 and ROUGE-L are not analysed here.
- The random baseline uses a single seed; its variance across seeds is not assessed.
- Results are for one dataset (MTSamples, cleaned) and one summary length (3 sentences).
